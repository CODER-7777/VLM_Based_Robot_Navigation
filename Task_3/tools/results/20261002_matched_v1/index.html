What the new data show

Stair ascent remains unsuccessful; jitter improvement is inconsistent. Twenty evaluations cover sixteen distinct conditions, including three matched starting poses. These are small deterministic comparisons, not a statistical success-rate estimate.

  1. The first foot hits the riser too low. In the central candidate run, front-left contact occurs at 9.730 s with foot-center height 5.63 cm; roughly 12.2 cm is needed to clear the 10 cm top with this foot radius. That leg's targets and torques are not clipped at the instant of contact. Inspect the event plot.
  2. The stair interaction amplifies rapid motion. Over matched six-second intervals, candidate joint 8–30 Hz RMS is 0.331° on flat versus 0.833° with stairs. This isolates the terrain's effect on the feedback loop, not one internal root cause. Compare the traces.
  3. Lower target removal does not guarantee less jitter. Speed tracking and target removal improve in the early-motion pairs, but joint oscillation changes have mixed signs. See every paired start.
  4. Observation timing matters. Refreshing observations changes outcomes but does not solve climbing. Next inspect timing, foot clearance, support transfer and heading before further reward or actuator changes.

Target limits do not change gear/belt ratios: changing hip/calf bounds does not require modifying J_LEG. The old audit is a hypothesis source, not ground truth. Derivation, code, measurement definitions and checklist.
